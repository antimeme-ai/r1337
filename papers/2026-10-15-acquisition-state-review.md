# Acquisition design review: engine state and failure semantics

Review date: 2026-10-15. Scope: the initial [ACQUISITION.md](../docs/ACQUISITION.md), specifically configuration/arming, output and capture state, draining/reuse, resource ownership and timing metadata. Read current instructions/doctrine, architecture fault contracts, previous architecture reviews, recent journals and the relevant measurement note. This judges detailed block/contract design before implementation; it does not require finished formats, selected components or schematics. No code/reference execution, builds, device operations, purchases, installations or new external research occurred. Only this report was edited.

The draft preserves the reviewed architecture's essential boundaries: local hardware owns timed execution, configurations must fit reserved resources, acknowledged state controls indication, incomplete captures remain explicit, and reset/power transitions require a real output-control mechanism. Clock resolution, threshold/synchronizer latency, shared-trigger delay and later drift are appropriately distinguished. Three contract details remain to resolve.

## S1 — High: output permissions and ownership are not tied to acquisition phases

**Affected sections:** “Proposed local engine behavior / Configure and arm” (draft lines 138–148), “Acquire and report” (152–156), and “Stop, reset and reconfigure” (171–183).

**Practical failure:** configure/arm sets directions and a stimulus sequence, but the document does not say which target-facing actions are permitted before the trigger. An implementation can begin driving during configuration even though the operator regards the instrument as merely armed. The reverse ambiguity occurs at completion: acquisition ends or the trigger wait expires, while a source or target-power output deliberately remains held. A host that treats the listed capture states as the whole engine state can release the interface, change a range/direction or begin another operation while the earlier output is still active. The general normal-end/host-loss policy does not define whether “end” means acquisition ended, stimulus ended or output no longer asserted.

**Proposed resolution:** require each mode to map its permitted target-facing output state to configuration, armed waiting, trigger/execution, acquisition end, timeout/cancellation and drain/reconfiguration. Configuration may deliberately establish power, bias or a defined drive level where needed, but that must be an explicit acknowledged part of the mode, not an accidental consequence of writing pin registers. Track output/transaction state separately from capture progress when they can differ. Resource ownership continues for any asserted/held output or executing transaction; capture completion alone does not release it. Reject incompatible reconfiguration until the previous mode reaches its defined transition condition. Do not impose “all outputs off until trigger” or require every output to stop when capture ends: those policies would be incorrect for some modes. Existing host-loss, unknown-outcome and physical reset/power controls remain mandatory.

## S2 — Medium: drain/re-arm/reset transitions do not define capture identity or buffer release

**Affected sections:** “Configure and arm” (138–148), “Acquire and report” (152–162), “Stop, reset and reconfigure” (177–183), and “Independence and contention” (191–194).

**Practical failure:** an ended capture is partly retrieved when the host reconnects, re-arms or changes the engine image. Delayed blocks or a retried indexed read can then be associated with the new capture/configuration, or an old buffer can be reused before its only retained contents have been consumed. Actual count/settings/reset-continuity fields help interpret a known record, but the draft does not bind each drain reply and buffer reservation to that record or say when reuse is permitted. An FPGA image change can also destroy otherwise valid data without a new acquisition overwriting it. This would violate the parent architecture's no-silent-overwrite rule even when neither capture overflows.

**Proposed resolution:** specify one capture/operation identity, including the relevant engine reset/configuration generation, to bind state, blocks and metadata. Pending reads from a prior generation cannot silently become reads of its successor. State the release rule: retained capture data remains protected until the host acknowledges the required retained portion or deliberately requests a recorded discard under the mode's policy. A planned reset/reconfiguration that would destroy unread data must drain it first or explicitly discard/mark it lost; an unexpected reset records loss/unknown availability rather than implying retrievability. Separate the reservation of that memory from the ownership of physical outputs. New capture and old drain may overlap only where distinct buffers/resources and their identities actually permit it. This needs no UUID format, transactional storage system or generalized job scheduler; the boundary and reuse conditions are the design requirement.

## S3 — Medium: configuration acknowledgment does not establish valid acquisition timing

**Affected sections:** “Configure and arm” (138–146), “Acquire and report” (158–167), and the source/reference path in “The complete path.”

**Practical failure:** the engine reports that a new range, reference clock or sample configuration was applied, then immediately declares itself armed. An analog frontend may still be settling, a clock may not yet be stable, or a conversion/filter pipeline may still contain samples from the preceding configuration. The record can be labeled with the new settings even though its initial samples represent the transition or an earlier mode. The existing frontend-latency requirement describes timing of established paths; it does not say when a newly configured path becomes valid.

**Proposed resolution:** make readiness for arming/acquisition distinct from merely accepting or applying register settings. Each affected mode defines the necessary frontend/clock readiness, settling and pipeline-flush/discard behavior, or preserves and marks an unsettled/invalid prefix with a clear validity boundary. Identify which local component enforces or confirms that condition; an explicit operator-established settling interval can be appropriate for a simple external frontend. If acquisition during a transition is itself the intended observation, retain the transition and its timing rather than describing the whole interval as steady operation under the final settings. Exact delays, lock indications and ADC details can remain open until actual hardware is selected.

## Disposition and scope limits

Resolve S1–S3 in the engine contract, then carry them into the later per-mode design and prototype qualification. No contradiction was found in the current clock-domain/trigger-delay/drift distinctions, and no new supervisor or remote-control machinery is needed. The existing architecture already governs retries and unknown target-action outcomes; this review does not replace it with a stricter blanket ban on diagnostic/stop operations.

Later direct checks should examine output state through configure/arm/timeout/drain, stale drain data and buffer reuse across reset/reconfiguration, and known stimuli across configuration/readiness boundaries. These checks address the identified failures. No tests or hardware operations are requested during the present documentation phase.

## Revision disposition — 2026-10-15

Re-read the revised “Proposed local engine behavior” and qualification sections against S1–S3. **All three findings are addressed at detailed block/contract-design maturity.** Original findings above remain the review history.

- **S1 — addressed:** the phase/output table now defines configuration, waiting, execution, timeout/cancellation, drain and reconfiguration requirements. Output/transaction progress is distinct from capture progress, acknowledged intentional bias/power/drive is explicit, and physical-output ownership persists through running or held states until compatible transfer/release. Capture completion no longer implies stopped outputs or released pins.
- **S2 — addressed:** status, reservations, metadata and drain blocks are bound to operation and reset/configuration generation. Explicit retention acknowledgment/discard governs completed-buffer release; planned destructive changes require drain or recorded loss, unexpected reset preserves lost/unknown status, and overlap requires distinct resources/identities. The circular pre-trigger exception is explicitly separate from completed-record protection.
- **S3 — addressed:** applied configuration is distinct from readiness; the mode assigns settling/clock/pipeline validity and failure behavior, with old-mode data discarded or an invalid prefix/first-valid boundary retained. Deliberate transition observation preserves changing configuration and timing.

The existing physical fault-control, host-loss and unknown-action contracts remain present. Qualification questions now cover the phase, stale-data, reuse and readiness failures directly. No requirement from this review remains missing; actual per-mode states, hardware paths, timing limits and their verification remain subsequent design/implementation work rather than demonstrated behavior.
