# Flipper aftermarket: actual additions and integration boundaries

Research date: **2026-10-15**. This is a categorical survey for r1337's integrated field instrument and optional Flipper companion, not a shopping list or bill of materials. Read current project instructions, architecture, capability matrix and inventory. Sources are original makers, app authors, component manufacturers and the Flipper catalog/documentation. Four pinned source/hardware archives and two original PDFs were acquired and read selectively; the [acquisition manifest](2026-10-15-flipper-aftermarket-acquisitions.md) records exact revisions, hashes, read scope and restoration. No source was executed, built or installed; no equipment, purchases or firmware changes occurred.

The meaningful additions are **different RF/protocol frontends, trusted credential-processing hardware, electrical adaptation, and task-specific sensing**. Much of the market instead improves antennas, output power, packaging or interaction around capabilities already represented by the stash. Neither type is intrinsically better value: compare the complete working capability, including host software, adapters, enclosure and support.

Evidence labels below distinguish **inspected implementation/design**, **maker-documented hardware/app**, and **unresolved integration**. None means measured performance on our unit. Current accessories and FAPs must not be assumed compatible with the recorded 2023 Flipper dev 0.81.1 installation.

## Resolve the recalled names first

**“nardsam” means NARD plus a SAM, not a radio chip.** [Killergeek's original NARD](https://github.com/killergeek/nard/tree/64c63042eeb1a115a46c5cb9e4e8a52923bb3845) is a dual contact SIM/SAM interface with published KiCad design. The Microchip SEC1210 is the bridge; the inserted HID SAM is a separate secure element holding provisioned cryptographic capabilities. Its schematic explicitly uses 5 V power, not the 3.3 V rail. NARD uses the PC0/PC1 serial pins, so its passthrough GPIO does not make those pins available to another simultaneous user. The UART-versus-USB SEC1210 variant matters.

[Microchip's SEC1110/SEC1210 datasheet, DS00001561E](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/OTH/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/00001561E.pdf) describes an on-chip 8051, smart-card timing/voltage handling, ISO/IEC 7816 contact interfaces and T=0/T=1 protocols. It supplies neither an NFC antenna nor UHF RF hardware. The chip's supply and card-voltage requirements are different from saying its host speaks 3.3 V logic.

**SAMadams is the current Red Team Tools replacement for its old NARD offering.** [The maker/seller page](https://www.redteamtools.com/sam-adams-for-flipper-zero/) identifies a smaller redesign with a soldered HID SAM and the Seader app. It explicitly says the supplied SAM is reader-configured, has no credential-encoding API, and does not break Seos cryptography. It can process supported standard-key/SIO credentials; this does not confer arbitrary customer-specific keys or a universal DESFire reader. It also explicitly says **SAMadams has no UHF reader**, even though the SAM can process UHF SIO data.

**“flippermeister” resolves to FlipperMeister, whose original design is Didgitalpunk's UHF-RFID/SAM interface board.** [Its published CAD/BOM/README](https://github.com/Didgitalpunk/UHF-RFID_SAM-interface_Board/tree/a9db9edc497dc1768d0f6be6ef2bac62b11ccd42) shows a carrier for an external UHF module, SEC1210/SAM interface and SPI/ICSP headers. The reader, provisioned SAM, sockets/soldered configuration, interconnect cable and mechanical parts determine the actual package. SPI/ICSP headers expose existing programming paths; they do not contain a second dedicated programmer. The project's public material includes custom reuse terms rather than a blanket open-hardware commercial license.

[KSEC's listing branded “RRG Flipper Meister Full Kit”](https://labs.ksec.co.uk/product/flipper-meister-full-kit-with-sam-uhf-module/) describes board, soldered SAM, UHF module, mounting hardware and cable. That branding does not establish authorship, identical revisions or identical SAM provisioning across sellers. The older RTT FlipperMeister URLs linked by Seader returned 404 during this pass; SAMadams' current page still refers to it. Exact currently deliverable FlipperMeister configuration remains a procurement/documentation question. Manufacturing allegations are outside this technical comparison.

## UHF RFID is the largest clearly separate radio capability

The [GS1 Gen2 air-interface standard](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/gen2/) describes a reader that supplies continuous-wave RF energy while receiving a passive tag's backscatter. Alternating reader/tag messages are not evidence that an ordinary switched transmit/receive packet radio can do the job. **Frequency overlap alone is insufficient:** CC1101 tuning near a tag band does not supply a Gen2 reader's RF separation, receive path, anti-collision inventory and protocol engine. A reader, tag emulator, passive sniffer and raw-I/Q receiver are different instruments.

Tag type, password and writable-bank properties govern what changes | UHF inventory/tag memory is new. Reader module is the capability; carrier adds power/mechanical convenience. App source is public, no local test |
| MTools FZUHF / UHF Expansion | Maker specifies 840–960 MHz, EPC Gen2/ISO 18000-6C, 3.3 V operation, UART and USB-C. Current app identifies UCM601/UCM601NC; this is not merely a YRM100 app with another label | Current pinned source 1.6 implements inventory, memory paging/read/write, selected protection actions and saved records; it does not demonstrate UHF tag emulation. Retail page still separates Flipper inventory from PC/Android writer features; catalog showed 1.5 | Genuine new band/protocol frontend. Public app implementation; reader firmware/RF design and actual hardware performance not audited. [Store](https://shop.mtoolstec.com/product/uhf-expansion-for-flipperzero/), [source](https://github.com/mtoolstec/fz-uhf-expansion/tree/8280ee566c4fcb44ddc2d3b0428c584e8a619b71), [catalog](https://catalog.flipperzero.one/application/6a89a0bb63013e0edb1f1883/page) |
| M6e Nano / M7e Hecto with Simultaneous UHF app | Separate ThingMagic reader plus the author's Raspberry Pi/Mercury-API bridge; YRM100 alternative has a direct serial path | Author documents multi-tag operation and bank operations; advertised 150 tags/s is specific to M6e/M7e and not our measurement. Bridge, reader region/power and exact API support matter | More capable inventory path may be useful on the core host, but includes another compute/software boundary. [App author](https://github.com/haffnerriley/Simultaneous-UHF-RFID-FlipperZero) |

Do not collapse all advertised UHF ranges into one standard guarantee. Vendor modules often say 840–960 or 860–960 MHz; the current GS1 document is Release 3.0.1, February 2026, whose stated scope is 860–930 MHz. Reader regional configuration and fitted antenna/matching remain separate from both numbers. [M5Stack's current U107 documentation](https://docs.m5stack.com/en/unit/Unit_UHF-RFID) is a concrete caution: it identifies JRD-4035, 840–960 MHz tuning, 18–26 dBm output, 5 V Grove power and a **922 MHz fixed antenna**, explicitly warning that range drops away from its antenna frequency. Its one-meter claim is conditional, not a transferable range for another module.

The acquired MTools source also exposes useful integration limits. New board revisions reserve C0 for reset; the app uses a 115200-baud USART control link, not an RF sample stream. Saved TID/User data is a bounded cached prefix, not proof of a complete memory-bank dump. Its store/README table labels Flipper pin 8 as 3.3 V, conflicting with the [official physical numbering](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/gpio-and-modules), which identifies 3.3 V on pin 9. Clarify exact connector orientation/revision before treating those tables as wiring instructions. This report deliberately supplies no connection recipe.

## HF/LF/contact credentials: processing, geometry and software vary independently

[Seader's inspected implementation](https://github.com/bettse/seader/tree/abc2a54c9d0c22c57b56c6a06459658fc1d7dd4a) uses the Flipper's NFC pollers for HF transactions and UART/CCID/T=1 handling for the SAM. Source paths `hf_interface_fal/hf.c`, `uart.c`, `ccid.c`, `sam_api.c` and `board_identity.c` make those boundaries concrete. SAM-only and UHF-carrier classes differ; UHF support also checks SAM capabilities. A second contact slot does not mean both cards or every app operate concurrently. Source includes a USB CCID passthrough for presenting the attached SAM to a PC, which is another host path rather than a new RF reader.

[MikroE Smart Card 2 Click](https://www.mikroe.com/smart-card-2-click) supplies a generic SEC1210 contact-card interface and is a documented Seader alternative. Its smart-card interface can support other contact-card work with suitable software; inserting a SIM does not turn Flipper into a cellular modem. Generic or unprovisioned secure elements do not substitute automatically for the required HID reader configuration.

| Path | What it adds | What it does not add |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current Flipper NFC/LF apps and writable/magic test tags | Protocol parsing, supported reads/writes/emulation using existing RF hardware; special tags change what that **tag** permits writing | App installation creates no new RF frontend or arbitrary credential keys. Supported roles vary by tag/protocol; consult [official NFC](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/nfc) and [LF documentation](https://docs.flipper.net/zero/rfid) |
| External PN532 controller boards | A separate 13.56 MHz antenna/controller and UART/SPI/I2C host interface; [NXP documents](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/brochure/939775017218.pdf) ISO14443 A/B, FeliCa, reader/writer and selected peer/card modes | A generic PN532 is not automatically a Flipper-native extension, universal sniffer or full substitute for Flipper's NFC stack. No complete PN532-to-Flipper integration was verified here |
| Passive NFC repeaters, replacement coils, larger external readers | Geometry/reach/serviceability changes; active readers can bring their own supported protocols | A passive loop is not a reader, amplifier, decryptor or raw capture instrument. A different coil needs matching and assembled-product testing; GPIO is not a native NFC antenna socket |
| Dedicated RFID instruments with a “Flipper controller app” | Potentially a separately powered/emulating instrument, controlled by Flipper | Cannot count the controller README as a working transport. The examined [Chameleon_Flipper project](https://github.com/muylder/Chameleon_Flipper/tree/2677b5735b18c41f569de7f93fd9690290ecbf98) has conflicting implemented/planned claims; its [“USB connection” handler](https://github.com/muylder/Chameleon_Flipper/blob/2677b5735b18c41f569de7f93fd9690290ecbf98/lib/uart_handler/uart_handler.c) uses Flipper USB-device CDC APIs, not a USB-host stack. A direct device-to-device cable is unestablished |
| Wiegand reader bridges such as RFIDThief | [Maker's architecture](https://www.phrack.me/hardware/2025/02/26/Flipper-Zero-RFIDThief.html) uses an ESP32-C3 UART/Wi-Fi bridge to a separate reader-side Wiegand device, plus display/NFC tag storage | “Long-range RFID” comes from a separate reader, not a new long-range LF/HF RF frontend on the bridge. This is a controlled reader/interface study path, not extra range from Flipper's own coil |

These paths can add useful credential interpretation and controlled test endpoints, but SAM-backed reading is not equivalent to writing or emulating the original protected credential. Existing Flipper LF/HF hardware already overlaps many basic roles.

DigiLab is unusually instructive because its actual schematic is available. The acquired Rev1.3.2 PDF shows a resistor divider/buffer/RC ADC path and TLV3501 comparator path, with shared grounds and 3.3 V bus headers. Inspected `401DigiLab_scope.c` uses `furi_hal_adc_read` in a periodic callback. Thus the advertised frequency indicator is not evidence of faithful waveform sampling to that frequency; a plotted trace does not establish sample-clock quality, analog bandwidth, differential/common-mode input capability or calibrated accuracy. Its probing ergonomics could nevertheless inform r1337's controls and probes.

## Multifunction boards and complete-package economics

[Rek5 QUAD](https://rek5lab.com/), [HoneyHoney Apex5](https://github.com/HoneyHoneyTeam/ESP32-Marauder-5G-Apex-5-Module---For-Flipper-Zero), Mayhem and Poltergeist illustrate different combinations. Apex5 documents C5 Wi-Fi, separate 433/868 sub-GHz modules, nRF24 and GPS; Mayhem emphasizes camera/PSRAM; Poltergeist combines C5, SD/OLED and switched IR. These combinations can be worthwhile mechanical integration. They do not establish concurrent operation, common timestamps, arbitrary frequency coverage or shared firmware support merely by occupying one PCB. Identify which MCU owns each peripheral, which antenna serves each band and which UART/SPI/reset/power resources conflict.

Observed prices illustrate why configurations matter: RTT SAMadams was **US$79**, MTools UHF Expansion **US$39.99**, Electronic Cats CANBus **US$49 with OBD cable**, its Modbus **US$20 board-only**, and Rabbit CAN Commander **US$149.99** on the pages read this date. These are materially different packages, not a ranking or like-for-like price comparison; tax/shipping, cases, cables, provisioned SAM, reader and adaptation may change the total. The Flipper itself is excluded. No FlipperMeister price comparison is reliable without resolving its exact available kit and SAM configuration.

For r1337, the leading **coverage gaps worth examining** are UHF reader access, the specific SAM/contact-card role if relevant to the lab, verified industrial electrical interfaces, and compact sensing/probing. Extra CC1101/Wi-Fi boards should earn their place through improved antenna placement, specific band/packet support, onboard retention or a useful independent counterpart. None is selected. The available Pi host may be a better direct controller for some modules; wrapping everything through Flipper would add transport and UI constraints without necessarily increasing capability.

## Important unresolved material for later acquisition/confirmation

- Exact presently deliverable FlipperMeister revision, fitted SAM model/configuration, reader module, cable pinout and current maker instructions; old RTT URLs are unavailable. Do not substitute another seller's title as a BOM.
- UCM601NC original module datasheet/schematic, exact FZUHF PCB pin numbering/revision and reader-firmware compatibility; reconcile store, catalog and source features. No original module RF/protocol manual was acquired.
- Current CAN Commander board schematic, port/termination/isolation details, board firmware and supported CANFD operations; app/source version must match actual hardware. The old MCP2515 project is insufficient documentation for the new board.
- Exact measured gain/filter/antenna/power information for any selected amplified radio, rather than multiplying advertised range claims. Generic nRF/CC1101 modules have many physical variants.
- Delivered sensor/probe models and Flipper firmware/API compatibility. No remote/support API, continuous raw-capture throughput or synchronized multi-module operation was demonstrated.

These are targeted documentation gaps for root's shopping/acquisition tracking, not purchase requests. The report's coverage is intentionally categorical: representative implementations plus niche paths, with duplicate cosmetic bundles omitted.
